Yield and Economic Losses with EpiExposure

Introduction

Disease severity alone does not fully describe the epidemiological consequences of an epidemic.

In many pathosystems, the final objective is to quantify how disease translates into reductions in crop yield and, ultimately, economic losses.

EpiExposure provides a framework that combines exposure-lag-response modeling with disease prediction and economic impact assessment.

This vignette demonstrates how simulated disease outcomes can be converted into:

  • yield loss;
  • remaining yield;
  • economic loss;
  • economic return scenarios.

These analyses allow researchers to evaluate the practical consequences of alternative environmental conditions.

Generate epidemiological scenarios

Yield losses are evaluated using predicted disease outcomes generated under alternative environmental conditions.

Create epidemiological periods:

periods <- define_periods(
  max_lag = 85,
  cuts = c(
    20,
    40,
    60
  )
)

Generate candidate scenarios:

scenario_grid <- simulate_ranges(
  periods = periods,
  vary = list(
    tmean = c(20,34),
    rain = seq(0,15),
    wetness = c(2,10)
  ),
  scenario_type = "grid",
  scenario_var = "rain"
)

Predict disease severity:

predictions <- simulate_scenarios(
  fit = fit,
  scenarios = scenario_grid,
  data = epi_data,
  uncertainty = TRUE,
  output = "summary",
  n_samples = 100
)

Converting disease severity into yield losses

Why estimate yield losses ?

Environmental conditions do not directly reduce yield.

Instead, environmental conditions influence disease development, which subsequently affects crop production.

EpiExposure uses user-supplied disease-loss relationships to estimate yield consequences.

Example damage function

Suppose disease severity and yield are related through:

Yield = a - b * Severity

where

• a = attainable yield; • b = disease damage coefficient.

For this example:

slope = 49.3
intercept = 9691

Estimate yield losses

losses <- simulate_losses(
  data = predictions,
  y = "prediction",
  slope = slope,
  intercept = intercept,
  attainable_yield = seq(
    4000,
    12000,
    by = 50
  ),
  price = 300
)

Interpretation

High disease pressure scenarios generally result in larger reductions in expected yield.

The magnitude of the loss depends jointly on:

  • environmental conditions;
  • predicted disease severity;
  • attainable crop yield.

Economic losses

Why estimate economic losses?

Yield reductions are useful biologically, but economic losses are often more relevant for management decisions.

Economic losses combine yield reductions with commodity prices.

Calculate economic losses

price = 300

Comparing management scenarios

Low disease pressure scenario

low_pressure <- losses %>%
  filter(
    rain == min(rain),
    wetness == min(wetness)
  )

High disease pressure scenario

high_pressure <- losses %>%
  filter(
    rain == max(rain),
    wetness == max(wetness)
  )

Compare distributions:

bind_rows(
  mutate(low_pressure,
         scenario = "Low pressure"),
  mutate(high_pressure,
         scenario = "High pressure")
) %>%
  ggplot(

      econ_loss,

Sensitivity to attainable yield

EpiExposure allows attainable yield to vary across a plausible range.

This is important because disease losses depend on production potential.

The same disease severity may generate very different economic outcomes under low- and high-yield production systems.

Example:

losses %>%
  ggplot(
    aes(
      att_yield,
      econ_loss
    )
  ) +

Pratical applications

Yield and economic loss simulations can be used to:

  • evaluate disease risk scenarios;
  • compare management strategies;
  • identify critical environmental conditions;
  • estimate economic consequences of epidemics;
  • support disease forecasting systems;
  • explore climate change impacts on crop production.

Summary

This section demonstrated how EpiExposure connects exposure-lag-response modeling with agronomic and economic outcomes.

The workflow consists of:

Exposure histories ↓ DLNM model ↓ Disease prediction ↓ Yield loss estimation ↓ Economic loss estimation

This framework allows researchers to move beyond disease prediction and evaluate the practical consequences of environmental conditions on agricultural production.
